Your nonprofit's website is often the first place a donor, volunteer, or community member encounters your organization. If it's confusing, slow, or hard to navigate on a phone, you're leaving impact on the table.

Here are five signs it's time to rebuild.

## 1. Your Donation Page Has a High Drop-Off Rate

If people land on your donation page but rarely complete a gift, the page itself is the problem. Common culprits: too many form fields, no trust signals (like security badges or testimonials), and a checkout flow that bounces users to an unfamiliar third-party processor with no visual continuity.

A well-designed donation flow is short, branded, and mobile-friendly. Every extra click costs you conversions.

## 2. It Doesn't Work on Mobile

More than half of web traffic is mobile. If your site was built before 2018 and was never redesigned with mobile in mind, it's almost certainly broken on phones — tiny text, buttons that are hard to tap, images that overflow the screen.

Test your site right now on your phone. If you wince, your donors are wincing too.

## 3. You Can't Update It Without Calling a Developer

Content should be easy to keep fresh. If updating your events calendar, adding a staff member, or posting a news item requires filing a ticket or waiting on a contractor, your CMS is failing you.

Modern headless or block-based CMS setups put content control in the hands of your team, not a dev queue.

## 4. It Fails Basic Accessibility Standards

Nonprofit organizations often serve communities with higher rates of disability. An inaccessible website is both an equity issue and a legal risk. Common failures include:

- Missing `alt` text on images
- Insufficient color contrast
- No keyboard navigation support
- Forms without proper labels

Run your site through the [WebAIM WAVE tool](https://wave.webaim.org/) for a free audit.

## 5. Your Bounce Rate Is Over 70%

A high bounce rate — visitors who land and immediately leave — usually signals one of three things: the page didn't match what they expected, it loaded too slowly, or the content didn't clearly answer "what does this organization do and why should I care?"

Your homepage should answer that question in the first three seconds, above the fold, without requiring a visitor to scroll or read a wall of text.

---

## What to Prioritize in a Redesign

If any of the above rings true, here's where to focus first:

1. **Mobile-first layout** — design for the smallest screen, then expand
2. **Streamlined donation flow** — fewer fields, clear CTAs, branded checkout
3. **Accessible design system** — WCAG 2.1 AA as a baseline, not an afterthought
4. **Editable content areas** — empower your staff with a real CMS
5. **Performance** — aim for under 3 seconds load time on a mid-range mobile device

A website redesign doesn't have to be a years-long project. With the right partner and a clear scope, organizations see meaningful improvements in donor conversion and volunteer sign-ups within months of launching a new site.
